# Computer Science Conference Style Reference

This reference supports the `venue-templates` skill for general computer science conference submissions.

For AI/ML/CV-specific conferences, also consult `ml_conference_style.md`.

Always verify current official venue instructions.

---

## 1. Scope

Use for general CS conferences and workshops where the style may be ACM, IEEE, Springer LNCS, USENIX, or custom LaTeX.

Examples:

- ACM-style conferences;
- IEEE conferences;
- Springer LNCS proceedings;
- USENIX-style venues;
- workshops with custom templates.

---

## 2. Common CS Formatting Concerns

Check:

- two-column or one-column format;
- anonymous vs non-anonymous review;
- page limit;
- reference page policy;
- appendix policy;
- font size and margin constraints;
- author block format;
- ACM CCS concepts or keywords;
- IEEE keywords;
- copyright block;
- DOI/copyright metadata;
- line numbering;
- artifact badges or reproducibility badges.

---

## 3. ACM-Style Notes

Common ACM requirements may include:

- `acmart` class;
- conference acronym;
- rights management fields;
- CCS concepts;
- keywords;
- ACM reference format;
- author metadata;
- anonymization mode for review.

Do not fabricate ACM metadata.

Use TODOs if exact conference information is unknown.

---

## 4. IEEE-Style Notes

Common IEEE requirements may include:

- `IEEEtran` class;
- two-column format;
- IEEE bibliography style;
- author block rules;
- abstract and keywords;
- PDF Express compliance for some venues;
- copyright notice for final version.

Do not change IEEE margins or fonts manually.

---

## 5. Springer LNCS Notes

Common LNCS requirements may include:

- `llncs` class;
- abstract;
- keywords;
- numbered sections;
- Springer bibliography style;
- author block and affiliations;
- page limit depending on venue.

Check official venue instructions because LNCS-based conferences vary.

---

## 6. Style vs Content Boundary

This reference may guide:

- section order;
- formatting;
- abstract/keywords placement;
- bibliography style;
- template-specific commands.

It should not decide:

- contribution wording;
- scientific claim strength;
- citation validity;
- result interpretation;
- figure creation.

Use the relevant neighboring skill.

---

## Final Reminder

CS conference style is template-driven.

Do not manually approximate a style when an official template exists.
