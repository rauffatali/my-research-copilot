# Venue Writing Styles Reference

This reference supports the `venue-templates` skill when identifying venue-level writing expectations.

For actual prose drafting, use `scientific-writing`.

For author/project style adaptation, use `prior-style-adapter`.

For claim support, use `claim-auditor`.

---

## 1. Purpose

Some venues have recognizable writing expectations.

This reference helps identify those expectations without letting style override evidence.

Venue style may affect:

- section emphasis;
- abstract shape;
- contribution framing;
- limitation placement;
- reporting expectations;
- audience assumptions.

It should not change scientific meaning or claim strength.

---

## 2. AI/ML/CV Conference Style

Often values:

- clear technical contribution;
- method/evaluation clarity;
- strong baselines;
- ablations;
- reproducibility;
- limitations;
- concise writing;
- direct claim-evidence alignment.

Avoid:

- broad hype;
- weak novelty framing;
- unsupported SOTA claims.

---

## 3. Applied AI / Domain Venue Style

Often values:

- domain motivation;
- practical relevance;
- data realism;
- task validity;
- domain-specific failure modes;
- deployment constraints;
- connection between metrics and domain needs.

Avoid:

- generic model application without domain insight;
- technical metrics disconnected from practical value.

---

## 4. Journal Style

Often values:

- complete reporting;
- clear context;
- transparent limitations;
- reproducibility;
- polished but precise prose;
- structured statements.

Check article type and journal instructions.

---

## 5. High-Impact Interdisciplinary Style

Often values:

- broad significance;
- concise explanation;
- strong figures;
- accessible framing;
- clear evidence chain.

Do not inflate impact.

Use `claim-auditor`.

---

## 6. Grant Style

Often values:

- significance;
- innovation;
- feasibility;
- clear aims;
- expected outcomes;
- risk mitigation;
- reviewer-friendly structure.

Do not invent preliminary results or commitments.

---

## 7. Style Handoffs

Use:

- `scientific-writing` for actual drafting;
- `prior-style-adapter` for author voice;
- `claim-auditor` for claim strength;
- `peer-review` for likely reviewer expectations.

---

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] Venue audience identified.
- [ ] Writing expectation identified.
- [ ] Scientific meaning preserved.
- [ ] Claim strength preserved or made safer.
- [ ] Author style needs routed to `prior-style-adapter`.
- [ ] Venue format needs routed to `venue-templates`.
- [ ] Final claims routed to `claim-auditor`.

---

## Final Reminder

Venue style helps communicate the work.

It should never make the work claim more than the evidence supports.
