# Poster Guidelines Reference

This reference supports the `venue-templates` skill for research posters.

Use it for poster size, layout, section planning, readability, and venue constraints.

For technical diagrams, use `scientific-schematics`.

For general images, use `generate-image`.

For result table scaffolds, use `results-scaffold`.

---

## 1. Purpose

A poster should communicate the research question, method, evidence, and conclusion quickly.

It is not a compressed full paper.

---

## 2. Venue Checks

Check:

- poster size;
- orientation;
- file format;
- print requirements;
- institutional template;
- font size;
- logo/branding rules;
- author/affiliation format;
- QR code rules;
- accessibility requirements;
- submission/upload deadline.

---

## 3. Common Poster Structure

A research poster may include:

- title;
- authors and affiliations;
- motivation/problem;
- research question;
- method;
- dataset/experiment;
- key results;
- interpretation;
- limitations;
- conclusion;
- QR code/link to paper/code;
- acknowledgments.

Use only what fits.

---

## 4. Readability

Good posters use:

- large fonts;
- short text blocks;
- clear section hierarchy;
- strong visual anchors;
- readable figures;
- simple tables;
- concise captions;
- enough whitespace.

Avoid:

- dense paragraphs;
- tiny tables;
- too many citations;
- full-paper text pasted into poster boxes;
- unsupported claims.

---

## 5. Visuals

Use visuals when they support understanding.

Use:

- method schematic;
- data examples;
- result figure;
- qualitative examples;
- key table;
- domain workflow.

Route visual creation:

- `scientific-schematics` for technical diagrams;
- `generate-image` for general visuals;
- `results-scaffold` for tables.

Venue Templates checks sizing and layout after visuals exist.

---

## 6. Poster Claim Safety

Posters often overclaim due to space constraints.

Use conservative wording:

- “preliminary results” when needed;
- “under the evaluated setting”;
- “planned evaluation” for placeholders;
- “TODO” for missing values.

Use `claim-auditor` for final claims.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Poster size/orientation verified.
- [ ] Venue or institutional template checked.
- [ ] Main message is clear.
- [ ] Text is concise.
- [ ] Figures/tables are readable.
- [ ] Claims are evidence-backed.
- [ ] Missing values are marked.
- [ ] Visual creation routed to correct skill.
- [ ] QR/link/citation policy checked.

---

## Final Reminder

A poster should be readable before it is comprehensive.

Venue constraints decide layout; evidence decides claims.
